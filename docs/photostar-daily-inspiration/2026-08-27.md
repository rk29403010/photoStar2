# PhotoStar2 Daily Inspiration — 27 August 2026 — A person changes through time

## Repo signal

`main` now has one new commit since the previous brief: `888e510` (`docs: add PhotoStar daily inspiration log (#39)`), which establishes the durable inspiration index. There are no new application-code commits after the 8 August segmentation-model-storage merge.

The current face pipeline is more interesting than the absence of new feature code. `src/services/faces/peopleResolution.ts` clusters ArcFace embeddings by comparing every incoming face to a single continuously updated centroid for each cluster. A face whose similarity to that centroid is below `0.72` is marked suggested. This is simple and understandable, but it treats a person as one point in embedding space even when the archive may contain them at age 5, 25, 55 and 80. Because clusters are built sequentially, results can also vary with input ordering.

## Best next idea — Lifetime Identity Anchors

PhotoStar should represent a person with several trusted visual anchors from different periods of their life rather than one averaged face centroid.

For example:

| Person | Identity anchors |
| --- | --- |
| Mum | childhood · teenager · young adult · middle age · later life |
| Dad | young adult · 1970s · 1990s · later life |

A newly detected face would be compared with the most plausible anchors, informed by the photograph's estimated date when available. A face from a photograph dated approximately 1960 should not be judged primarily against that person's 2015 appearance.

This is not merely a UI improvement. It changes the recognition model from:

`face -> one person centroid`

to:

`face -> several era/appearance anchors -> person`

### Why this is supported by current research

Cross-age face recognition is a recognised failure mode of ordinary face recognition. MTLFace explicitly separates identity-related and age-related facial information and provides official PyTorch code and a project page:

- Official project: https://hzzone.github.io/MTLFace/
- Official code: https://github.com/Hzzone/MTLFace

MTLFace is from CVPR 2021/TPAMI 2022, so I would not integrate it blindly as a modern production dependency. Its relevance is the demonstrated principle that identity and age variation should not be collapsed into one undifferentiated representation.

SapiensID (CVPR 2025) is more recent and broadens recognition beyond the face to human appearance and body cues across pose, scale and long-term change. The authors released pretrained models and official code:

- Paper: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Kim_SapiensID_Foundation_for_Human_Recognition_CVPR_2025_paper.html
- Official code/models: https://github.com/mk-minchul/sapiensid

I found no author-hosted interactive SapiensID demo; the repository provides runnable inference/evaluation code and pretrained models.

Long-term person-reidentification research also reinforces the problem. DeepChange deliberately tests identity across changing clothes, hair and time, while DKC (CVPR 2025) tackles lifelong recognition without relying solely on clothing:

- DeepChange official project/data: https://github.com/PengBoXiangShang/deepchange
- DKC official code: https://github.com/PKU-ICST-MIPL/DKC-CVPR2025

Neither is a drop-in family-photo recogniser and both come from surveillance/person-ReID research, so they should be treated as research evidence and benchmarking candidates, not as an authority for family identity. I found no usable official hosted demo for either.

### Product value

This could improve some of the hardest family-archive cases:

- joining childhood and adult photographs of the same person;
- preventing a later-life face from pulling a person's centroid away from earlier photographs;
- using approximate dates to reduce false matches between visually similar relatives;
- handling profile/partial faces with supporting body and event context;
- selecting restoration reference photographs from an age-appropriate period.

### Likely effort

**Medium** for a useful first version. It does not require changing the face detector or ArcFace recogniser initially.

A minimal implementation could retain existing embeddings and assignments but replace one centroid with a small set of medoids/prototypes per confirmed person. Date-aware routing can arrive afterwards.

## Practical improvement — make identity anchors visible and editable

The People view should eventually expose a compact "Across their life" strip rather than only one representative face.

Example:

`1950s [face]  1960s [face]  1980s [face]  2000s [face]  2020s [face]`

The user can mark an especially clear face as an **identity anchor**. PhotoStar can also propose anchors automatically by choosing diverse, high-quality confirmed faces.

This solves two problems at once: the recognition system gets strong references, and the user gets a much more useful visual summary of a person than a single thumbnail.

**Effort: Low-medium** after multi-anchor matching exists.

## Review UX — Bridge the gaps, not every face

Current products still largely ask users to confirm individual suggestions. digiKam detects and recognises faces and places uncertain results into an unconfirmed queue; Mylio similarly uses local face recognition and tagging; Immich groups detected faces into people and lets users merge identities and assign a date of birth.

References:

- digiKam face workflow: https://docs.digikam.org/en/maintenance_tools/maintenance_faces.html
- Immich facial recognition: https://docs.immich.app/features/facial-recognition/
- Mylio face recognition: https://mylio.com/face-recognition/

PhotoStar could make review more strategic:

> **Possible missing period for Mum: 1963–1971**
> 18 uncertain faces could connect her teenage cluster to her adult cluster.

Show perhaps the three strongest bridge candidates rather than 18 separate face chores.

Confirming one or two bridge images can then unlock the rest.

**Effort: Medium**, but the interaction is much less tedious than a conventional unknown-faces inbox.

## Use dates as recognition evidence

Immich currently allows a person's date of birth to be stored so their age can be displayed at the time a photograph was taken. PhotoStar can turn that idea into recognition evidence rather than presentation only.

If a person was born in 1942 and a photograph is strongly dated to 1953, PhotoStar knows it is looking for that person around age 11. That should influence which identity anchor is consulted.

Conversely, face evidence can help check dates:

> Photograph estimate says 1982, but every trusted identity anchor matching this face is from the person's childhood. Review date or identity.

Neither side should silently override the other; it should create an explainable conflict.

This fits particularly well with the earlier Date Workbench / Claims and Evidence ideas.

**Effort: Medium** once date ranges can be consumed consistently by face resolution.

## Experimental model path — body-assisted identity, only where it adds evidence

SapiensID is worth a Model Lab experiment because it recognises people using face and body information across pose and scale, rather than requiring a clean frontal face. That is relevant to old family photos where faces are tiny, blurred or turned away.

But I would impose a strict boundary:

- use body appearance strongly **within the same event/short period**, where clothing is genuinely informative;
- use it only weakly across years;
- never infer a family identity from clothing alone;
- keep the face/manual/context evidence separately explainable.

A sensible first benchmark is therefore not "replace ArcFace". It is:

> On photographs where ArcFace is weak or has no usable face, does SapiensID retrieve the correct already-known person among the top few candidates?

**Effort: Low to benchmark; medium-high to integrate.**

## Restoration connection — choose references from the right age

The earlier reference-assisted restoration idea becomes safer if identity references are age-aware.

For a damaged photograph of someone aged roughly 12, PhotoStar should prefer clear photographs of them at 10–15 over pristine photographs from age 60. Later-life images may still help confirm identity, but should not dominate facial detail reconstruction.

This gives Lifetime Identity Anchors a second consumer beyond recognition and makes the data model more worthwhile.

**Effort: Low once both systems exist.**

## Creative concept — The Life Strip

Nicholas Nixon's *The Brown Sisters* has photographed the same four sisters annually since 1975, preserving the same left-to-right order. The power comes from repetition and the visible passage of time rather than from generated storytelling.

- MoMA overview: https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/1484
- Smithsonian discussion: https://americanart.si.edu/blog/eye-level/2016/09/358/nicholas-nixons-brown-sisters

PhotoStar could automatically construct a **Life Strip** for an ordinary family member from photographs that already exist.

It would select perhaps one strong, roughly comparable portrait per year or per few years, align crop/eye position for viewing only, and present:

`1954 -> 1959 -> 1965 -> 1972 -> 1981 -> 1994 -> 2008 -> 2025`

No morphing. No synthetic age progression. No invented intermediate face.

The interesting variations are themselves historical: hairstyle, expression, glasses, partners, illness, work, fashions and changing photographic technology.

Allow the user to lock a preferred image for a year and let PhotoStar fill the rest automatically.

**Effort: Low-medium** once dates and identity anchors are available. **Creative value: high.**

## Best next experiment

Do not replace ArcFace yet.

Take perhaps 10 people for whom the archive spans decades. For each:

1. manually confirm a representative set of faces across their lifetime;
2. compare the current single-centroid approach against 3–5 era/diverse prototypes;
3. hide some confirmed faces and test whether the correct person is recovered at top-1/top-3;
4. break the results down by age gap, face size and pose;
5. only then test MTLFace or SapiensID if the simple multi-prototype approach leaves a meaningful gap.

The first experiment may show that **the current ArcFace embeddings are already good enough if PhotoStar stops averaging a lifetime into one face**.

That would be the best outcome: a meaningful recognition improvement driven mostly by a better archive model rather than another heavyweight AI dependency.
