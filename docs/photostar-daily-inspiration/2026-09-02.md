## Best next idea — Place Solver

PhotoStar should infer *where* an old photograph was taken by combining weak evidence rather than asking one model to guess a pin.

Useful evidence can include:

- visual place recognition against other known family photographs;
- OCR from signs, shop fronts, street names and station boards;
- album/roll adjacency and event grouping;
- known family addresses and relatives' places;
- date range;
- architecture, landscape and landmarks;
- historical maps and gazetteers;
- a user's remembered constraint such as “somewhere near Retford”.

The output should be an explainable region or ranked candidate places, not false GPS precision.

Example:

> **Probably Retford town centre, 1965–1972**
>
> - same shopfront appears in two already-located photographs;
> - OCR suggests `Carolgate`;
> - this frame is adjacent to photographs identified as Retford;
> - 1960s OS mapping contains a matching street junction.
>
> **Confidence: strong, exact camera position unresolved.**

This extends the earlier Place Threads idea from grouping known places into actually *solving unknown places*.

## Why this is timely

Current products demonstrate demand but mostly solve only parts of the problem:

- **RetroTagr** now offers AI-assisted geotagging of old/scanned photographs, including multi-photo analysis and manual refinement: https://www.retrotagr.com/
- **Past Viewer** is map-first historical-photo browsing with timeline filters and then/now camera comparison: https://pastviewer.com/
- **GhostViewer**, launched in 2026, focuses on rephotography and aligning historical images with modern views: https://ghostviewer.co.uk/
- **TheGenealogist MapExplorer** added large-scale 25-inch historic OS mapping and LiDAR in February 2026.
- **LiminArc** combines historical maps, LiDAR and heritage records for genealogy/local-history landscape research: https://liminarc.com/

PhotoStar's opportunity is to connect those ideas to private family evidence and uncertainty/provenance rather than merely writing a guessed EXIF coordinate.

## Model Lab candidate — GeoCLIP

**GeoCLIP** learns a shared representation between images and geographic coordinates and can return top candidate GPS locations worldwide.

Official code and demo links are in the authors' repository:
https://github.com/VicenteVivan/geo-clip

It is MIT licensed and installable as a Python package. Treat it as a candidate generator, not an authority. Family photographs are often indoor, historical and unlike modern geotagged training imagery, so benchmark it on real PhotoStar material before integration.

## Better local retrieval candidate — MegaLoc / AnyLoc

For many family archives, the question is not “where on Earth?” but:

> “Is this the same street, house, church, beach or viewpoint as another photograph we already know?”

That is visual place recognition (VPR), and may be more reliable and useful than global geolocation.

- **MegaLoc (CVPR 2025 Workshop)** provides official code, model weights and a Gradio demo: https://github.com/gmberton/MegaLoc
- **AnyLoc** provides official code, standalone demos, notebooks and a Gradio app for universal visual place recognition: https://github.com/AnyLoc/AnyLoc

The first PhotoStar experiment should compare these against the app's existing generic image embeddings on difficult same-place/different-decade examples.

## Historical-map layer — use open standards

**Allmaps** is especially attractive because it is open source, TypeScript-based and built around georeferenced IIIF maps from libraries and archives. It provides packages for MapLibre, OpenLayers and Leaflet, and publishes georeference annotations as open data.

Official code: https://github.com/allmaps/allmaps
Live editor/demo: https://editor.allmaps.org/

The National Library of Scotland is one of the supported map sources and currently exposes over 400,000 high-resolution historical map images covering the UK and Ireland among other areas.

For PhotoStar, this means an old OS map could be overlaid without building a GIS stack from scratch.

## UI — Location Workbench

For an unlocated photo, show evidence in one compact view:

| Evidence | Contribution |
| --- | --- |
| Related photographs | Same house appears in 4 located images |
| OCR | `...gate` visible on sign |
| People/event | Family known to live near Retford |
| Historical map | Matching junction/building footprint |
| Visual model | Candidate: Retford / Worksop / Newark |

The user can then accept:

- exact pin;
- named place only;
- approximate region;
- reject all suggestions.

Never silently convert an AI guess into normal camera GPS metadata. Keep observed GPS, human assignment and estimated location distinct.

## Practical feature — “Find where this was”

One button on a photograph:

**Find where this was**

PhotoStar first searches the user's own archive for same-place matches. Only then does it escalate to OCR, maps and optional external/model-assisted geolocation.

That order minimizes cost, privacy exposure and hallucination while exploiting the strongest unique information PhotoStar has: the rest of the family archive.

## Creative concept — Stand Here

Once a historical photograph has a plausible camera position and direction, PhotoStar can create a rephotography task:

> **Stand Here**
>
> Approximate viewpoint: 12 m ahead, facing NW
>
> Overlay the old photograph translucently on the phone camera until major edges align.

The resulting modern photograph is linked to the original as a **rephotograph**, not merely a similar image.

Then a family place can become a time sequence:

`1937 → 1964 → 1992 → 2026`

This develops Place Threads into an active way of adding new evidence to the archive.

GhostViewer shows that the core then/now alignment interaction is already understandable to users; PhotoStar's differentiator would be automatic linkage to family people, events, dates and historical maps.

## What to test next

Use 50 photographs whose locations are already known but hide that metadata:

1. 20 with another known photograph of the same place in the archive;
2. 10 containing useful text/signage;
3. 10 recognisable public landmarks/streets;
4. 10 genuinely hard family interiors or generic landscapes.

Measure separately:

- own-archive VPR top-1/top-5;
- GeoCLIP candidate usefulness;
- OCR contribution;
- combined evidence outcome.

Do **not** start with a map UI. First determine whether PhotoStar can recover useful place evidence.

The core principle is:

> **Solve location as an evidence problem, not a pin-prediction problem.**
