# PhotoStar2 Daily Inspiration — 3 September 2026 — Identity Solver

## Repo signal

`main` still has no application-code commits after 8 August 2026. The later 26 August commit only established the durable daily-inspiration log.

The useful architectural gap today is that PhotoStar already has two capable but separate systems:

- `src/services/faces/peopleResolution.ts` clusters ArcFace embeddings and persists `people` / `face_assignments`.
- `src/services/gedcom/relationshipService.ts` builds an explicit family graph with parent, child and spouse paths plus birth/death dates.

The current face clustering treats recognition mainly as a visual problem. The family graph is not being used to help answer the much more useful question: **who could this unknown person realistically be?**

## ★ Best next idea — Identity Solver

For an unidentified face, PhotoStar should combine visual similarity with archive context and genealogy to produce a small, explainable candidate list.

Example:

> **Unknown woman — probably photographed 1958–1962**
>
> 1. Jean Smith — strong face match; age would be 19–23; appears with two confirmed siblings in this photo.
> 2. Margaret Smith — moderate face match; age would be 42–46; same family branch, but rarely appears with this group.
> 3. Elsie Brown — weaker face match; age plausible; appears repeatedly in nearby album pages.
>
> **Best candidate: Jean Smith — 82% support**
>
> Evidence: face similarity · date/age fit · co-occurrence · family relationship · album context

The point is not the percentage. The point is that every candidate has reasons the user can inspect and correct.

**Effort:** Medium  
**User value:** Very high, especially for mystery relatives in older albums.

## Use hard constraints before clever AI

PhotoStar can eliminate many impossible candidates deterministically:

- Person was not yet born or had already died at the estimated photo date.
- Candidate age is implausible for the visible person.
- The same confirmed person already appears elsewhere in the same image.
- Candidate belongs to a clearly incompatible generation when the date is well constrained.

Then use softer evidence:

- ArcFace / Lifetime Identity Anchor similarity.
- Who else is present.
- Family-tree relationship to confirmed people in the image.
- Repeated co-occurrence across nearby photos or events.
- Same album/roll/location.
- Clothing/body appearance within one event.

This is likely to outperform another raw face model on family photographs because the search space becomes dramatically smaller.

## Current product signal

MyHeritage's Photo Tagger already groups similar faces and, for deceased people, can automatically suggest names from the user's family tree based on previously tagged photographs. Its July 2026 Photo Discoveries feature goes further by connecting photographs found elsewhere to people already in the tree, while keeping human review before acceptance.

- https://blog.myheritage.com/2022/09/new-photo-tagger-now-available-on-the-myheritage-website/
- https://www.myheritage.com/help/en/articles/12852411-what-are-photo-discoveries

FamilySearch's Compare-a-Face compares a supplied face with ancestor portraits up to six generations, which validates user interest in visual family resemblance, although it should not be treated as identity or biological proof.

- https://www.familysearch.org/en/help/helpcenter/article/how-do-i-compare-my-photo-to-my-ancestors

The opportunity for PhotoStar is to combine the strengths of both ideas while keeping the reasoning local and explicit.

## Research support — context helps recognition

The CVPR paper **A Multi-Level Contextual Model for Person Recognition in Photo Albums** showed that person recognition improves when face appearance is combined with body/clothing, identities of other people in the same photograph and shared context across groups of photos.

- Official paper page: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2016/html/Li_A_Multi-Level_Contextual_CVPR_2016_paper.html

I could not find a maintained official code release or usable author-hosted demo for this work. The paper is older, but the design principle maps unusually well to PhotoStar's domain.

## Experimental signal — kinship resemblance

There is also active research on **kinship verification**: estimating whether two faces may depict biological relatives. The 2025 Forest Neural Network work provides official code and reports improvements on KinFaceW benchmarks.

- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18910
- Official code: https://github.com/ali-nazari/Kinship-Verification

The KinFaceW project itself explicitly lists family-album organisation and genealogy among potential applications:

- https://www.kinfacew.com/

PhotoStar should **not** use a kinship model to assert biological relationships or identities. At most, Model Lab could test whether it provides a tiny ranking signal such as "this unknown face looks more consistent with this family branch than that one". Genealogy records and human confirmation remain authoritative.

A useful confidence-calibration reference is **Confidence-Calibrated Face and Kinship Verification**, whose authors provide official code for converting raw similarity scores into better-calibrated confidence estimates:

- https://github.com/cnulab/ASC

No hosted interactive demo was found.

## UI — Mystery Person Casefile

Instead of a generic "Unknown Person" card, open a casefile:

### Unknown Person 17

**Seen in:** 12 photographs  
**Likely period:** 1956–1968  
**Often with:** Jean · Peter · Margaret  
**Likely family branch:** Smith  
**Candidate identities:** 4

Then show candidate cards with evidence toggles:

| Candidate | Face | Date/age | Co-occurrence | Tree context |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Jean Smith | Strong | Strong | Strong | Strong |
| Margaret Smith | Medium | Medium | Weak | Strong |
| Elsie Brown | Weak | Strong | Medium | Weak |

One confirmation should propagate through the whole cluster and update related cases.

**Effort:** Low-medium once the solver exists.

## Negative evidence is valuable

PhotoStar should explicitly record reasons *against* a candidate, not just supporting signals.

Examples:

- "Jean is already the confirmed face on the left."
- "Margaret died in 1953; this photograph is strongly dated after 1958."
- "Peter would be approximately 7 years old, but this face is clearly an adult."

That makes incorrect automation much easier to spot and turns identity resolution into an auditable process rather than a mysterious classifier.

## Creative concept — Family Branch Lens

For a mystery face, temporarily project the relevant family branch around it using actual photographs:

```text
        grandmother      grandfather
             \           /
              mother — father
                  \     /
                [unknown]
                  |
          siblings / cousins
```

Faces are real archive portraits, preferably age-appropriate Lifetime Identity Anchors. Strong supporting links brighten; contradictory candidates fade.

The 2025 Hood Museum exhibition **Visual Kinship** is a useful conceptual warning as well as inspiration: photography can represent biological family, but also migration, adoption, chosen family and other forms of belonging. So this visualisation should distinguish **documented genealogy**, **social/photo co-occurrence** and **visual resemblance** rather than collapsing all three into "family".

- https://hoodmuseum.dartmouth.edu/explore/exhibitions/visual-kinship

**Effort:** Medium later  
**Creative value:** High.

## Best implementation order

1. Create an explicit bridge between PhotoStar `people` records and GEDCOM persons; allow "unlinked" on both sides.
2. Build deterministic candidate filtering from birth/death dates and duplicate-in-photo constraints.
3. Add co-occurrence and album/event context.
4. Add visual similarity from existing embeddings / Lifetime Identity Anchors.
5. Present the top 3–5 candidates with positive and negative evidence.
6. Only then benchmark specialist kinship/context models in Model Lab.

## What I'd test next

Take 50 already-known faces and temporarily hide their names.

Compare:

- **A:** current face similarity only;
- **B:** face similarity + date/age filtering;
- **C:** B + co-occurrence + GEDCOM relationship context.

Measure whether the correct person appears in the top 1 and top 3 candidates.

If C materially beats A, this deserves first-class architecture without requiring any new large model.

The strongest principle today is:

> **For old family photographs, identity is not just a face-recognition problem. It is an evidence-constrained search problem over a family archive.**
