## Best next idea — Film Strip Archaeology

PhotoStar should treat negatives/slides as historical source objects rather than inconvenient images that first need to be converted into positives.

For a strip or sheet scan/camera capture, PhotoStar could:

- detect individual frames while retaining the complete uncropped strip as evidence;
- preserve perforations, frame numbers, manufacturer/stock markings and unexposed rebate;
- infer frame order and likely roll membership;
- create positive representations non-destructively;
- match a negative exposure to existing scans/prints of the same photograph;
- use adjacent frames to improve event/roll reconstruction;
- keep film-stock/edge-code dating as evidence with explicit uncertainty rather than converting it directly to a photo date.

**User value: very high where negatives survive. Effort: medium for a useful first pass.**

### Semantic model fit

Useful entity/relationship candidates include:

- `FilmStrip` / `FilmRoll`
- `Exposure`
- `Representation` (negative scan, positive conversion, print scan, restoration)
- `adjacent_to`
- `same_exposure_as`
- `part_of_roll`
- `derived_from`
- propositions such as `film_stock = Kodak ...` or `manufactured_in ~= 196x`, each backed by edge-mark evidence.

The physical-source image remains untouched; conversions and crops are representations of it.

## Why not just add negative conversion?

Current specialist software is already good at inversion. Negative Lab Pro remains a mature Lightroom workflow; FilmLab supports desktop/mobile digitisation and proof sheets. More interestingly for PhotoStar, the open-source **DarkSlide** now combines local negative conversion, batch processing, contact-sheet generation, high-bit-depth TIFF/PNG export and an experimental dust/scratch workflow in a React/Tauri/Rust stack. Its repository links an official live browser demo.

- Negative Lab Pro: https://www.negativelabpro.com/
- FilmLab: https://www.filmlabapp.com/
- **DarkSlide official code + live demo link:** https://github.com/kilianvivien/DarkSlide

Another open-source reference, **OpenEnlarge**, performs negative inversion in density space using a physically motivated Cineon/Beer–Lambert approach, explicitly sampling the clear film base/rebate rather than treating inversion as a generic tone-curve flip. It exposes a headless CLI, which makes it especially suitable for a PhotoStar Model Lab comparison.

- **OpenEnlarge official code:** https://github.com/mohaelder/openenlarge

No separate author-hosted upload demo was found beyond the project/software interfaces documented by these repositories.

The PhotoStar opportunity therefore is not to beat dedicated darkroom software. It is to understand the **relationships around the film**.

## Practical feature — Strip Ingest

A low-friction workflow:

1. Import or photograph the whole strip/sleeve/page.
2. Auto-detect frame boundaries.
3. Show a contact-sheet preview.
4. Let the user correct only bad frame splits.
5. Store the full strip plus frame-level regions.
6. Generate draft positive representations.
7. Search PhotoStar for existing positive/print versions of each exposure.

UI result:

> **Film strip — probable 35 mm, 24 frames**  
> 21 frames separated automatically · 2 uncertain boundaries · 14 likely matches to existing photographs

This avoids forcing users to scan/crop/name every frame before PhotoStar can help.

## Edge evidence — useful, but don't overclaim

Kodak's official KEYKODE material shows why film-edge information is worth preserving: machine- and human-readable edge numbers encode film identity/frame indexing and support exact matching back to original negative material.

- Kodak KEYKODE: https://www.kodak.com/en/motion/page/keykode-numbers/

For older cine film, East Anglian Film Archive material documents Kodak edge/date codes and warns that codes indicate **manufacturing date**, not necessarily exposure date, and that some symbol series repeat over decades. That distinction is exactly what PhotoStar's Claims & Evidence design should retain.

- East Anglian Film Archive edge-code reference: https://www.filmforever.org/edgecodes.html

Still-camera film edge markings are less uniformly date-decodable, so PhotoStar should treat OCR/edge interpretation as a candidate clue rather than promise automatic dating.

## Material extension — Roll Reconstruction becomes evidence-based

Earlier Roll Reconstruction inferred likely shooting sequence from scanned photographs. Surviving film strips make some of that structure directly observable.

PhotoStar can distinguish:

- **observed adjacency** — exposures physically adjacent on one surviving strip;
- **strong roll inference** — separate strips share stock/rebate/numbering and compatible sequence;
- **weak reconstructed sequence** — derived from content/date/context only.

That hierarchy prevents an AI-inferred ordering from looking equivalent to actual film evidence.

It also improves Event Stitcher: same people/outfits/place across consecutive exposures is much stronger evidence of one event when the negatives are physically consecutive.

## UI/UX — Contact Sheet mode, but not another global gallery

The normal masonry gallery should stay unchanged. When the user enters a known roll/strip, offer a local **Contact Sheet** view preserving physical order, including blank, blurred and apparently unimportant frames.

Those "bad" frames can matter historically: they may show how a scene evolved, identify the photographer, reveal an otherwise unseen person, or explain why one frame was selected for printing.

The Morgan Library's current **Hujar:Contact** exhibition (22 May–25 October 2026) uses more than 110 contact sheets from a collection of over 5,700 to show precisely this: contact sheets expose sequence, cropping/selection decisions and the photographer's working process.

- Morgan Library — Hujar:Contact: https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/hujar-contact

This is a useful design reference rather than a product template.

## Matching negatives to prints — the highest-value automation

A particularly practical button:

> **Find positive versions of these frames**

PhotoStar searches the existing archive for crops/scans/prints representing the same exposure despite:

- negative vs positive polarity;
- different crops;
- rotation/mirroring;
- fading and colour shifts;
- print borders;
- restoration edits.

Once confirmed, knowledge can flow at the `Photograph/Exposure` level while every representation retains its provenance.

**Effort: medium. Value: very high.** It can eliminate duplicate cataloguing and attach a newly discovered high-quality negative to years of existing metadata.

## Model Lab experiment — polarity-invariant same-exposure retrieval

Do not assume ordinary CLIP embeddings will handle negative/positive pairs well.

Build a small gold set of known pairs and compare:

1. generic image embedding on raw scans;
2. generic embedding after deterministic negative inversion;
3. perceptual hashes/features after grayscale/contrast normalisation;
4. local feature matching on cropped content;
5. ensemble ranking.

A very recent BMVC 2026 paper, **Composed Historical Image Retrieval by Modeling Temporal Representations (TDIR)**, is relevant conceptually because it separates temporal and content components so historical-photo retrieval can vary one dimension while retaining another. It was posted 19 August 2026 and is explicitly evaluated on historical photographs.

- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18694

I could **not find an official author code repository or hosted demo**; third-party "request code" pages are not evidence of a release. So treat the paper as a research direction, not an immediately testable dependency.

A second July 2026 study on CLIP failures in historical photo collections is a useful warning: visual coherence and text-image alignment can fail independently, and fine-tuning gains differ by concept type. Again, historical material deserves a PhotoStar-specific gold set instead of assuming benchmark behaviour transfers.

- Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19836

I could not verify a maintained author-provided code/demo for that work either.

## Creative concept — The Unprinted Story

Once a roll is reconstructed, distinguish:

- frames known to have surviving prints;
- frames that exist only as negatives;
- frames apparently rejected by the original photographer/family.

Then offer an optional story view:

> **The photographs they kept — and the moments between them**

A printed family portrait might sit beside the two exposures immediately before and after it. The "failed" frame where somebody laughs or turns away can make the preserved portrait feel like a moment in time rather than an isolated icon.

Do **not** label unprinted frames as rejected unless there is evidence; "no matching print found" is the safe claim.

This concept is inspired by contact-sheet practice rather than generated content. The Morgan's current Hujar exhibition is particularly relevant because selection marks and adjacent exposures reveal the process behind final photographs.
