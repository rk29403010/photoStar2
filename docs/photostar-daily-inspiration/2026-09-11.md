# PhotoStar2 Daily Inspiration — 11 September 2026 — Album Page Graph

## Repo signal

The meaningful movement is on PR #42 rather than `main`. WP9 is now closed and WP10 has started the face cutover. The current branch head (`d7f62e11`) introduces durable semantic `Face` and `VisualRegion` identities, persists region geometry generations, and links analysis-owned masks to stable `visualRegionId` values.

That makes the earlier **Album Archaeology** idea materially more implementable: album photos, captions and other page elements can now have stable region identity while semantic propositions describe what those regions mean.

## ★ Best next idea — Album Page Graph

When PhotoStar ingests a full album or scrapbook page, preserve the entire page as a first-class historical representation **and** detect meaningful regions inside it:

- mounted photographs;
- handwritten/typed captions;
- decorative elements;
- tickets/cards/stamps;
- empty or removed-photo areas;
- headings/page numbers.

Each region receives a stable identity. Extracted photographs become representations linked back to the exact source-page region.

The key difference from ordinary multi-photo scanning is that PhotoStar does **not discard the original page layout after cropping**.

Useful relationships include:

- photo A is beside caption B;
- photos A–D were mounted on the same page;
- caption B probably describes photo A;
- region C appears to be a missing-photo mount.

These should remain observations/propositions with provenance and uncertainty.

**Effort:** Medium.
**User value:** Very high for inherited albums.

## UI — Show in original page

Every extracted photo should have:

> **Show in original page**

PhotoStar zooms out to the untouched page scan with that region highlighted.

Conversely, clicking a region on the page opens the extracted photograph or attached evidence.

This avoids the usual trade-off between convenient cropped images and preservation of page context.

## Caption Association

Use geometry before language-model guesswork:

1. detect text/handwriting regions;
2. preserve their coordinates;
3. OCR/HTR them separately;
4. propose photo-caption links using proximity/alignment/page structure;
5. ask for one-click confirmation.

A handwritten line such as “Mum at Yarmouth, 1957” should remain:

- its original handwriting crop;
- a transcript;
- a proposed relationship to a photo;
- a human decision.

Do not silently flatten it into photo metadata.

## Model Lab — DocLayout-YOLO

OpenDataLab's **DocLayout-YOLO** is an official open-source layout detector aimed at finding document regions at varying scales.

Official code:
https://github.com/opendatalab/DocLayout-YOLO

Official demo:
https://huggingface.co/spaces/opendatalab/DocLayout-YOLO

It is trained for documents rather than family albums, so use it only as a baseline.

A useful 2025 historical-document study, **From Codicology to Code**, found that oriented bounding boxes materially improve irregular historical-page layout detection.

Paper:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20326

I found no clearly author-provided runnable codebase or hosted demo for that specific study, so treat the result as a design clue rather than an implementation dependency.

## Preservation rule

The UK National Archives' digitisation guidance stresses capturing full content/context and the entirety of pages, while bound material should be imaged safely rather than forced flat.

- https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/information-management/manage-information/preserving-digital-records/digitisation/
- https://www.archives.gov/preservation/family-archives/digitizing

PhotoStar should preserve:

1. full/raw page capture;
2. corrected page representation;
3. extracted regions;
4. restorations.

Never replace the first with the third.

## Creative concept — Family Editor's Desk

Once pages are structured, PhotoStar can expose the family's original act of curation:

- photographs chosen together;
- handwritten captions;
- later notes in a different hand;
- decorative placement;
- blank mounts where something is missing.

The Met's **Harvey Album** demonstrates how page decoration and portrait placement add meaning beyond the individual print:
https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/282247

The Met's exhibition of Berenice Abbott's 1929 New York album similarly treated the arrangement of 266 prints across 32 pages as evidence of creative process:
https://www.metmuseum.org/press-releases/berenice-abbott-2023-exhibitions

PhotoStar should make the narrow historical claim:

> **These photographs were physically arranged together.**

Why they were arranged together may remain unknown.

## Experimental concept — Missing Slot

If PhotoStar detects a clearly shaped empty mount, photo corners or adhesive residue, create a **missing-photo region**.

Then search loose/scanned photos for plausible candidates using aspect ratio/border, same event/people, nearby album chronology and visual similarity.

Present:

> **Possible photograph originally mounted here**

with evidence and uncertainty.

## Best next experiment

Test:

1. photo-region detection;
2. caption/text-region detection;
3. stable region identity;
4. one-click crop ↔ source-page navigation.

Success criteria:

- captions remain independently attributable evidence.

## Recommendation

**Album Page Graph is today's best next idea.**

This is a material development of Album Archaeology rather than a repeat: the new WP10 `VisualRegion` work now supplies a clean implementation primitive for durable album-page regions and evidence-linked relationships.

> **Extract the photographs for convenience, but never throw away the family's original arrangement of them.**
