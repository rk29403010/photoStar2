# PhotoStar2 Daily Inspiration — 15 September 2026 — The Invisible Photographer

## Best next idea: Photographer Solver

Family archives preserve who is in front of the camera far better than who was behind it. PhotoStar should treat `who probably took this photograph?` as a reviewable proposition supported by evidence, not metadata to guess silently.

Useful evidence can include:

- a person repeatedly absent from a coherent roll/event while their close family are present;
- camera swaps: A appears in one frame, B disappears, then B appears and A disappears;
- mirrors/windows containing a photographer reflection;
- photographer shadows;
- known camera ownership, album ownership or handwriting;
- physical roll order and event/place/date evidence;
- a self-timer/remote possibility, which must prevent overconfident attribution.

The first version needs no new AI model. For a known roll/event, score plausible photographers from existing face identities, sequence and family context, then ask for confirmation only when the evidence is useful.

## UX: Behind the Camera

On a photograph or reconstructed roll:

> **Likely photographer: Margaret**
> Evidence: absent from 17/19 frames; appears immediately after a likely camera swap; same event/date.
>
> **Margaret · Someone else · Self-timer · Unsure**

The UI should expose the evidence rather than a bare confidence percentage. A person page could later offer **Photos taken by Margaret** separately from **Photos of Margaret**.

## Why it fits PhotoStar2 now

PR #42 has completed the local and remote Phase-1 semantic validation and is in publication handoff. The branch has durable propositions/evidence/decisions and Photograph/VisualRegion semantics, so photographer attribution can naturally be represented as a proposition with supporting/contradicting evidence instead of becoming another brittle field on an asset.

A particularly useful current constraint is that interactive vector candidate generation remains deferred: sqlite-vec measured about 508 ms p95 at 250k 512-d vectors versus the 150 ms target. Photographer Solver does not depend on solving that ANN problem; its first useful version can work over bounded roll/event groups.

## Current-product scan

Current mainstream/family tools continue to concentrate on the visible contents of photographs. Immich 3.2.0 (10 Sep 2026) adds Search v2, people improvements, map viewport browsing and memories. MyHeritage's 2026 Scribe AI analyses visual/historical clues, while Photo Discoveries connects photographs to people in family trees. These are useful adjacent capabilities, but the reviewed public feature descriptions do not expose inferred photographer identity as a first-class family-history relationship.

That is the product gap: PhotoStar can model not only who a photograph depicts, but who created the family record.

## Research/technical references

Egocentric-vision research demonstrates the complementary idea that the camera wearer can be characterised from the people, places and objects repeatedly seen from their viewpoint. Menchon et al., *Behavioural Pattern Discovery from Collections of Egocentric Photo-Streams*, analysed more than 100k first-person images using contextual patterns. I found the author/institution paper pages but no clearly maintained author-provided runnable demo/codebase for this specific method, so it is conceptual support rather than an implementation dependency.

For explicit visual evidence, reflection/shadow detection can be an optional later signal. The 3DRef project provides an author project page, dataset, scripts and pretrained reflection-detection resources, but it is aimed at reflective surfaces/robotics rather than identifying photographers and has no useful family-photo upload demo. DSDNet's author project page provides code/models for shadow detection; again this should be benchmark material, not an assumed solution.

## Storytelling concept: The Person Who Wasn't There

A family event page could optionally include a small section:

> **Behind the camera**
> Margaret probably made 18 of these 21 photographs. She appears in only three, after apparent camera swaps.

That changes the interpretation of an archive without generating fiction. The person least visible in an album may have shaped how the family's history looks.

Vivian Maier's self-portraits are a useful artistic reference: mirrors and shop windows reveal the otherwise hidden photographer inside her own photographic record. The official Vivian Maier site documents exhibitions specifically built around those reflected self-portraits.

## Experimental extension: Photographer Signature

Once some photographer attributions are confirmed, PhotoStar could test whether photographers have recurring, non-biometric habits:

- typical framing and camera height;
- subjects/people photographed disproportionately often;
- portrait vs landscape preference;
- recurring distance from subjects;
- tendency to centre/off-centre people;
- preferred viewpoints at familiar places.

This must remain weak evidence. It would be interesting primarily when combined with roll sequence and known absences, never as a standalone claim that a composition `proves` authorship.

## Best experiment

Use 10-20 rolls/events where the photographer is partly known from family knowledge. Build only a rule-based baseline:

1. reconstruct participating people per frame;
2. find people consistently absent while close family/group members are present;
3. detect likely camera-swap boundaries from appearance/absence changes;
4. rank plausible photographers;
5. compare against known answers;
6. record `self-timer/unknown` explicitly.

Success criterion: does the correct photographer appear in the top two candidates often enough that a one-click confirmation is useful?

If yes, **Photographer Solver / Behind the Camera** is worth developing before adding reflection or photographic-style models. It is cheap, uses the new semantic architecture directly, strengthens Roll Reconstruction and Event Stitcher, and recovers a historically important relationship that ordinary face-centric photo software largely leaves invisible.
