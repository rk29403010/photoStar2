## Best next idea — Camera Lineage

Treat the camera/scanner that created a representation as potentially useful family-history evidence. Build a lightweight `CaptureSource` concept from strong metadata first (make/model/serial, lens, scanner/software tags), then optionally test forensic source-camera fingerprints such as PRNU for born-digital originals.

User value:
- group otherwise-undated digital photos made by the same physical camera;
- infer chronology from known camera ownership periods without turning that into an exact date;
- strengthen Photographer Solver and event/roll reconstruction;
- distinguish camera originals, scans, exports and edited derivatives;
- expose a human story: “photos made with Dad’s Canon” rather than only “photos of Dad”.

Effort: low for metadata clustering; medium for capture-source entities/evidence; experimental/high-risk for PRNU.

## Research / Model Lab

**PRNU-Bench** (Croitoru, Hondru, Ionescu, 2025) provides an official author repository and code for device-level camera identification using photo-response non-uniformity. Official code: https://github.com/CroitoruAlin/PRNU-Bench ; paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17581 . No author-hosted interactive upload demo was found.

A useful warning comes from **PRNULeaks** (Montibeller et al.): non-unique processing artifacts can contaminate camera fingerprints and create false positives. Official author code: https://github.com/MMLab-unitn/PRNULeaks-IHMMSec24 . No hosted interactive demo was found.

Recent forensic work also shows PRNU reliability changes with exposure and image processing/compression. Therefore PRNU should be weak/inspectable evidence, never a silent camera identity authority.

## Product / archive synthesis

Current family-photo managers such as Tonfotos organize by people, dates, events and places, while EXIF-oriented organizers use camera metadata operationally. PhotoStar can make the source device historically meaningful by connecting it to people, periods, events and representations.

Museum collections reinforce the storytelling value of the photographic apparatus itself: the Science Museum and Smithsonian catalogue cameras as historical objects with makers, dates, formats and provenance. A family camera can likewise be part of the family archive rather than invisible technical metadata.

## Creative concept — Camera Biography

A camera page could become a miniature history:

> Dad’s Kodak / Canon / phone
> first observed photograph → holidays → family events → last observed photograph

Include confirmed owner(s), observed usage range, film/digital format, example photographs and uncertain gaps. This gives the photographer/capture process a place in the family story without pretending the device attribution is stronger than its evidence.

## Experimental concept — Camera Clock Solver

For digital cameras with systematically wrong clocks, estimate a time offset from independently dated anchor photos. If a camera is consistently +1 year / -43 minutes / wrong timezone, propose a correction model for that capture source. Preserve the recorded EXIF timestamp and store the corrected chronology as a derived proposition. This could repair hundreds of dates from one human confirmation.
