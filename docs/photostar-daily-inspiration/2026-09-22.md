## Best next idea — Archive Negative / Coverage Map

PhotoStar is increasingly good at understanding what is present. It should also help expose what the surviving archive does **not** contain.

Build a coverage model across time × people × places × capture sources, distinguishing three very different states:

1. photographs exist and are understood;
2. photographs exist but are undated/unidentified/unreviewed;
3. there appears to be a genuine gap in surviving material.

A Person page could show a compact lifetime strip with dense, sparse and unknown periods. A family-wide Coverage view could answer questions such as: "Why are there almost no photographs from 1989–1992?", "Which years of Margaret's childhood are represented only by one photograph?", or "Dad's Canon is present every year from 2008–2013 except 2011 — are files missing from ingestion?"

This is not a completeness score: absence can be historically meaningful, and PhotoStar must not imply photographs *should* exist.

**Value:** high for large/inherited archives and preservation work.  
**Effort:** low-medium for counts/visualisation; medium for evidence-backed gap hypotheses.

## UI — Coverage Lens

Add an optional `Coverage` mode to Timeline/Person/Place views. Avoid a red/green quality dashboard. Use neutral density/fog:

- solid/dense: well represented;
- thin: sparse surviving material;
- hatched: material exists but chronology/identity is unresolved;
- blank: genuinely unknown.

Clicking a sparse interval should explain why PhotoStar noticed it and offer actions such as `Show nearby photos`, `Search Undated`, `Check other sources`, or `Ignore gap`.

A particularly useful integration with Temporal Triage: if 80 Undated photos plausibly belong inside an apparent 1970s gap, show **unresolved coverage**, not a false archival absence.

## Practical feature — Source Coverage

Camera Lineage makes a cheap first implementation possible. Plot each known source/device/import root over time and detect discontinuities. A missing year from one camera can prompt a search of disconnected drives/import folders rather than historical speculation.

Example: `Dad's Canon: 2008–2010 | gap | 2012–2013`. If filesystem/import evidence suggests an omitted folder or card dump, that becomes a preservation task. If nothing supports that interpretation, leave it as an observation only.

## Change-point detection as a tool, not truth

The maintained `ruptures` library provides official implementations of offline change-point detection methods such as PELT, binary segmentation and kernel methods. Official code: https://github.com/deepcharles/ruptures ; official documentation: https://centre-borelli.github.io/ruptures-docs/ . There is no author-hosted PhotoStar-style interactive demo; the code/docs are the usable resources.

A simple monthly vector — photo count, identified-person count, known-source count, event count, undated candidates — could help flag abrupt collection-regime changes. But the output should read `coverage changes here`, not `photos are missing here`.

## Product comparison

Current family-photo tools mostly optimise the material that exists. Mylio emphasises unified libraries, decluttering and fast retrieval; Tonfotos combines timeline/people/family-tree/map navigation; Memmie groups family material into Moments and timelines; Memrial's Archive Checkup surfaces photos still missing names/dates/stories. Useful references:

- https://mylio.com/
- https://tonfotos.com/
- https://www.memmie.net/
- https://memrial.com/family-history-timeline

PhotoStar can add a different layer: **is this sparse because the archive is incomplete, because knowledge is unresolved, or because this period simply was not photographed much?**

## Creative concept — The Quiet Years

Allow a Story Cut or Person story to preserve gaps rather than smoothing them away. Between 1967 and 1974, for example, the story might deliberately show a quiet band saying only: `Few surviving photographs are currently known from these years.`

That is more truthful than filling the interval with generic historical context or generated imagery.

Art/curatorial precedent supports treating absence as content. Véronique Kolber's *Absences* makes missing family images visible through the traces left by removed frames (https://www.veroniquekolber.com/moving-through-time). ESEA Contemporary's *Voicing the Archive* explicitly attends to archival silences rather than pretending to fill them (https://www.eseacontemporary.org/voicing-the-archive). These are conceptual references, not software dependencies.

## Experimental concept — Never Seen Here

Borrow the *discovery* principle, not the implementation, from the Science Museum Group's `Never Been Seen`, which deliberately surfaces digitised collection objects that had received zero online views: https://blog.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/never-been-seen/ .

PhotoStar could offer `Show me something I've probably overlooked`: choose photographs that are technically ingested but have never been opened, favourited, annotated, included in a story, or connected to accepted knowledge. This turns coverage from a negative audit into gentle rediscovery.

Importantly, `never viewed in PhotoStar` is an application observation, not a claim that the family has literally never seen the photograph.

## Best next experiment

Use one real archive with known uneven coverage. Build only a derived month/year matrix from existing dates, people, source/import roots and unresolved-date candidates. No new schema and no AI model.

Test whether it can correctly distinguish:

- a true sparse period;
- an apparent gap filled by Undated candidates;
- a source/import discontinuity;
- a normal low-photography period that should simply be left alone.

The success criterion is not finding lots of gaps. It is whether the top 10 surfaced intervals produce useful actions — locate missing files, date existing photos, ask a relative, or consciously record that the surviving archive is sparse.

## Recommendation

**Archive Negative / Coverage Map is the best next idea to explore.** It materially extends Temporal Triage, Camera Lineage, Preservation Health and Story Cut without needing another heavyweight model. More importantly, it adds a capability most photo systems avoid: PhotoStar can reason carefully about the boundary between **missing knowledge, missing files and genuinely missing history** without inventing what is not there.
