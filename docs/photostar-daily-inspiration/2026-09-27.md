# PhotoStar2 Daily Inspiration — 27 September 2026 — Album Grammar

## Best next idea: Album Grammar

Treat a scanned physical album page as a first-class historical object, not merely a temporary container from which individual photographs are cropped.

PhotoStar should preserve the page image, positions and rotations of photographs, handwritten/printed captions, decorations and page order, while linking each extracted photograph back to its exact region on the source page. The album creator's arrangement is evidence: adjacency, sequencing, caption placement and deliberate juxtaposition can say things that disappear when photographs become independent JPEGs.

### Why it fits PhotoStar

The semantic/evidence work on PR #42 gives a natural model: an album page is source evidence; detected regions and OCR are observations; claims such as “this caption refers to these two photographs” remain propositions until accepted. It also complements Film Strip Archaeology, Temporal Triage, Story Cut and Photo Conversations without duplicating them.

### Practical v1

1. Import full-page scan/photo and keep it immutable.
2. Detect individual photograph regions and create/link representations.
3. Detect text/handwriting regions but do not silently assign them.
4. Preserve page number/order, geometry and rotations.
5. Provide a two-way link: extracted photo → “View in album”; album page → click any photo.
6. Offer a tiny review queue for uncertain crop/caption associations.

The first useful version needs no generative AI.

### UI: View in album

A normal photograph gets a small provenance action: **View in album**. It opens the page with that photograph highlighted. A page view lets the user move through the album as the original compiler intended, while clicking a photograph enters PhotoStar's richer person/place/event view. This gives large collections two complementary navigation modes: archival/original order and reconstructed semantic order.

### Why preserve layout

Current album-digitisation products often focus on extraction. ScanCropper detects and crops multiple photographs from full album scans; Sunday Album detects/splits photographs and adds glare removal, colour restoration and perspective correction; AlbumLift similarly detects, restores and documents individual crops. Photokive and UK service PicSave explicitly preserve intact album/page context, and DocMint preserves intended reading order. The useful synthesis for PhotoStar is to keep both representations permanently connected rather than choosing between page facsimile and individual images.

### Model Lab: Surya 2

Surya is an open document-intelligence toolkit supporting layout analysis, reading order and OCR in 90+ languages. The official repository includes runnable code and a local Streamlit interactive app (`surya_gui`). Its current model is designed for documents rather than natural photographs, which makes it interesting specifically for album-page structure/captions rather than photo understanding.

Official code/demo instructions: https://github.com/datalab-to/surya

Test it on 50 real album pages for text-region detection and reading order. Do not assume its generic `Picture` regions will crop family photographs well enough; benchmark that separately against PhotoStar's existing segmentation/crop tools or a small album-specific detector.

### Alternative layout baseline: DocLayout-YOLO

The official OpenDataLab DocLayout-YOLO implementation provides pretrained document-layout detection and links to an official Hugging Face demo. It is trained for general document elements rather than family albums, so use it as a cheap baseline, not as an architectural commitment.

Official code: https://github.com/opendatalab/DocLayout-YOLO

### Practical improvement: Caption tethering

When OCR finds “Cromer 1964” underneath two photographs, PhotoStar should show the text in place and ask once which image(s) it describes. One confirmation can then provide place/date evidence to multiple photographs while preserving the exact source wording and page coordinates. Handwritten text that OCR cannot confidently read should still be retained as a region for later transcription or Photo Conversations.

### Large-library navigation: Original journeys

Add an optional **Albums** route alongside semantic navigation. It should feel like turning pages, not another grid. A user can browse the historical artefact in original order and jump outward into people/events/places, then return to the same page position. This prevents semantic organisation from erasing the experience of the physical collection.

### Creative concept: The Editor Before Us

An album creator already made a Story Cut decades ago. Their choices — what was included, omitted, placed together, captioned, enlarged, rotated or given an entire page — form a human-authored narrative layer. PhotoStar can surface that gently: “This photograph appears alone on a page”; “these four photographs were mounted together”; “the same person appears on three consecutive pages.” Do not infer emotion or intent automatically; expose the observable editorial decisions.

A useful artistic precedent is current museum interest in archives as constructed sequences rather than bags of images. The Rijksmuseum's 2026 Ed van der Elsken exhibition used contact prints, notes, darkroom experiments and book dummies to reveal how selection and sequencing formed the finished work; the Whitney's 2026 Andy Warhol Family Album exhibition similarly presents hundreds of Polaroids from one of Warhol's assembled personal albums. These reinforce the idea that arrangement itself can be historically meaningful.

### Experimental concept: Album disagreement

Compare the album's implied grouping/order with PhotoStar's reconstructed chronology. If a page labelled 1964 contains one photograph whose independent evidence strongly indicates 1967, don't silently force either answer. Surface: **Album context says 1964; independent evidence suggests later. Review.** Conversely, a reliable album caption can become valuable dating evidence for otherwise undated photographs.

### Best next experiment

Use 20–50 scanned album pages. Preserve page scans, manually establish ground-truth photo boxes/captions, then compare current PhotoStar extraction with Surya/DocLayout-style layout signals. Measure crop recall, caption-region recall, correct caption-to-photo associations, and — most importantly — whether every extracted image can navigate losslessly back to its physical context.

Success is not perfect OCR. It is: **can PhotoStar extract photographs for modern search without destroying the album as a historical artefact?**

## Recommendation

Album Grammar is the best next idea to explore. It is practical, archival rather than gimmicky, strengthens existing chronology/evidence features, and turns information PhotoStar would otherwise discard — page position, sequence and captions — into durable provenance. The design rule should be: **separate the photographs for analysis; never separate them from the context in which somebody chose to preserve them.**
