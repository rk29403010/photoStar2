# PhotoStar2 Daily Inspiration — 28 September 2026 — The Other Side

## Best next idea: The Other Side

Treat the physical front and back of a photographic print as two representations of one historical object. A scan of the reverse should never become an unrelated image or a disposable OCR input: it can contain names, dates, places, studio stamps, processing marks, sequence numbers, dedications and later family annotations that materially change what the front means.

This is adjacent to yesterday's Album Grammar but solves a different preservation problem: Album Grammar retains page context; The Other Side retains the physical print's own two-sided provenance even after it has been removed from an album or scanned separately.

### Why it fits PhotoStar

PR #42's semantic kernel is a strong match. The front and verso are representations of one Photograph/physical-print entity; a handwritten inscription is source evidence; OCR/HTR is an observation; extracted claims such as “Susie Carey” or “Cromer 1964” are propositions until reviewed. Confidence must not silently elect truth.

Museum catalogues already treat verso evidence this way. Smithsonian records explicitly describe handwritten names, dates, locations, photographer information, stamps and personal messages on the reverse of photographs rather than treating the back as a second artwork.

Examples:
- https://nmaahc.si.edu/object/nmaahc_2014.282
- https://nmaahc.si.edu/object/nmaahc_2015.132.210
- https://nmaahc.si.edu/object/nmaahc_2012.163.2

### Practical v1: Pair front/back

1. Add a representation role: `front`, `verso`, `album-page`, `derived/restored`, etc.
2. Let the user pair two existing scans with a single **This is the back** action.
3. During import, suggest likely front/back pairs from filename adjacency, dimensions, scan order and capture time, but require review when uncertain.
4. Preserve the full verso image unchanged.
5. Detect text/stamp regions and offer transcription; do not overwrite the source wording.
6. Show a small flip control in the viewer rather than polluting the main gallery with backs as independent photographs.

A current commercial example shows the workflow is useful: Timeline Tagger displays each scanned photograph beside its handwritten back so a user can enter date/place, while Timeline Scan's FrontBack Scanner is designed to pair fronts and backs during scanning. The Memories Vault similarly links reverse scans to the correct front rather than delivering an unstructured folder of backs.

Sources:
- https://timelinescan.com/timeline-tagger/
- https://thememoriesvault.co.uk/photo-back-scanning

### UI: Flip the print

A photograph with a verso gets a discreet **↻ Back** control. Flipping should preserve zoom/context and show the actual reverse, with detected regions lightly indicated only on request. A side panel can separate:

- **Original writing** — literal transcription, including uncertainty.
- **What it may tell us** — proposed people/date/place/studio claims.
- **Accepted knowledge** — only human-confirmed or otherwise accepted propositions.

That avoids the common mistake of turning OCR output into metadata truth.

### Practical improvement: Verso triage

For a large scan job, do not ask the user to read every back. Rank only potentially useful reverses:

- handwriting/stamp detected;
- date-like expression detected;
- person/place names detected;
- studio/processor mark detected;
- multiple annotation hands/inks;
- conflicting information versus current PhotoStar knowledge.

Then offer a queue such as **42 backs worth reading**, rather than 2,000 backs.

### Model Lab: ExpertHTR

ExpertHTR is a very recent (11 September 2026) page-level handwriting-recognition system using multi-task learning and a sparse mixture-of-experts architecture. The authors report improvements across six of seven handwriting sources and state-of-the-art performance on the IAM paragraph benchmark.

Official paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12705
Official author code: https://github.com/DAIR-Group/ExpertHTR
Official model: https://huggingface.co/DAIR-Group/ExpertHTR

The official repository includes inference/training code and `hf_space_demo` Gradio demo code. I could verify the demo implementation in the authors' repository, but not a separately live hosted demo endpoint today, so treat it as runnable official demo code rather than claiming a live service.

For PhotoStar, benchmark it on actual photograph backs rather than assuming document-benchmark results transfer. Family-photo backs are unusually awkward: short fragments, multiple orientations, pencil, biro, fading, stamps, isolated names and writing over textured photographic paper.

### Alternative Model Lab baseline: CHURRO

Stanford's CHURRO is an open-weight 3B VLM specialised for historical printed and handwritten text, trained from a broad historical corpus. It is larger than ExpertHTR but offers a useful independent baseline and a clean Python/CLI toolkit.

Official paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19768
Official code/toolkit: https://github.com/stanford-oval/Churro
Official project/docs: https://stanford-oval.github.io/Churro/

The authors provide code, model/dataset links and runnable inference tooling. I did not find a useful official hosted upload demo; the official CLI/API is the reliable test route.

### Stronger feature: handwriting identity as evidence

Once PhotoStar has several confirmed inscriptions from the same relative, preserve **who wrote this?** as a separate proposition from **what does it say?**. Even a tentative handwriting match can be historically useful: an undated note in Grandma's hand means something different from a later annotation added by Robin.

Do not make writer identification automatic truth. Initially, let the user label known hands and use visual similarity only to generate candidates: **These 18 backs may be written by Margaret**.

This creates a new evidence dimension that complements Photo Conversations: spoken testimony and written testimony can both remain attributable to the person who supplied them.

### Large-library navigation: Written by

A Person page could eventually include a small **Written by** section alongside photographs/stories. It would expose inscriptions, postcards, album captions and notes attributable to that person, each linked back to the physical source image. This turns handwriting into a route through the archive without creating a separate document-management application.

### Creative concept: Voices in Ink

Create a story view composed only from genuine family photographs and the handwriting found on their backs. The front fills the screen; then the print flips and the original words appear in place, optionally accompanied by a careful transcription. No generated handwriting and no synthetic voice are needed.

A sequence such as “Cromer 1964” → “To Mum, love Jean” → “Dad's new car” can become a tiny first-person documentary layer authored by the family itself. The design should privilege the actual marks, spelling, crossings-out and uncertainty rather than polishing everything into modern prose.

### Experimental concept: Annotation archaeology

A single print may have accumulated information at different times: original studio stamp, contemporary pencil note, later biro identification, modern archive number. PhotoStar could preserve separate annotation regions and let a human say **same hand / different hand / probably later**. Combined with ink/position/style cues, this creates a chronology of how the family itself interpreted the photograph.

The key discipline is not to infer dates from ink colour or handwriting style as fact. Those cues are observations that can support propositions.

### Best next experiment

Take 100–200 scanned photo backs with a mixture of handwriting, stamps and blanks. Manually establish front/back pairs and literal transcriptions. Compare current OCR, ExpertHTR and CHURRO on the real material, measuring:

- useful-text detection (can blank/uninteresting backs be suppressed?);
- literal transcription accuracy;
- names/date/place extraction after transcription;
- false confident readings;
- review time per useful back.

The first product test is even cheaper: implement front/back pairing plus **Flip** with no AI and see whether the physical relationship feels natural in the library model.

## Recommendation

The Other Side is the best next idea to explore. It is small enough to ship incrementally, strengthens Photograph/Representation semantics in PR #42, gives Temporal Triage and identity/place reasoning unusually strong human-authored evidence, and prevents a very common digitisation loss: preserving the picture while detaching the words somebody wrote on the object itself.

Design rule: **a photograph's back is not metadata about the image; it is source material belonging to the same physical historical object.**
