## Best next idea — Sequence Memory

PhotoStar should preserve and reconstruct *photographic sequences*, not merely group similar pictures.

A run of adjacent negatives, slide numbers, scanner filenames, camera timestamps or near-consecutive digital frames often contains evidence that disappears when every photograph is treated independently. One weak frame may establish the place; the next may contain a dated sign; another may reveal who was holding the camera; together they may identify an event and constrain chronology for the whole sequence.

The user-facing primitive should be **Show neighbours**: from any photograph, reveal the most plausible frames immediately before and after it, with the reason for each link visible. Confirmed sequence membership can then support Temporal Triage, Photographer Solver, Place Solver, Album Grammar and Archive Negative.

Effort: low-medium for metadata-first grouping; medium for evidence-backed reconstruction of broken/scanned film sequences; high for AI-only ordering.

## Why this is more than stacking

Adobe Lightroom's current Assisted Culling can auto-stack by capture time or visual similarity and choose a representative. That is useful clutter reduction, but PhotoStar should retain a different concept: a sequence is evidence, not merely a pile whose lesser frames can be hidden.

For historical material, sequence clues can include filename/scan adjacency, EXIF time, camera identity, film-edge/frame numbers where visible, shared people/place/background, gradual viewpoint change and album/contact-sheet position. Each clue should be an attestation toward a `sequence_precedes`/membership proposition rather than silently rewriting dates.

## UI — Show neighbours

The viewer gets a small strip:

`← likely previous | current | likely next →`

Each candidate can expose *Why?*: `scan numbers adjacent`, `same camera + 18 sec`, `shared scene`, or `human confirmed`. A sequence page can expand to a contact-sheet-like view with gaps shown explicitly rather than compressed away.

This is useful for large collections because it creates local navigation: photograph → neighbouring moment → event, without returning to the global gallery.

## Practical improvement — sequence-level evidence propagation

Do not copy metadata blindly across a sequence. Instead let one frame contribute evidence to neighbours with declining strength and explicit scope.

Example: a frame with a clearly readable `Cromer` sign supports the proposition that nearby frames in the same confirmed roll sequence were also at Cromer, but does not prove it. A dated processing envelope may constrain a whole roll; a handwritten album caption may constrain only a mounted subset. PR #42's support/oppose attestations are well suited to expressing this without turning inference into truth.

## Research warning — do not ask an LLM to sort the roll

The ACL 2025 TempVS benchmark (`yjsong22/TempVS`) directly tests image ordering and event-relation reasoning. Its authors found a substantial gap between multimodal models and humans on temporal sequence reasoning. The authors provide the official Apache-2.0 benchmark/code and Hugging Face data from the repository; there is no PhotoStar-style hosted interactive demo.

A newer August 2026 paper, *Order Matters: LVLMs as Judges for Temporal Reasoning in Image Sequences*, reports strong primacy/recency and positional biases and argues that current LVLM judges are structurally weak at sequence-order discrimination. I could find the official paper, but no author-provided usable code repository or demo; third-party paper indexes only offer code requests.

That makes the product direction clearer: deterministic/physical sequence evidence first, embeddings second, VLM reasoning only as weak candidate evidence.

## Model Lab — TIME10k / timeline-vlm

The official `TekayaNidham/timeline-vlm` implementation of *A Matter of Time* provides the TIME10k benchmark and methods for extracting a low-dimensional temporal structure from VLM embeddings. It includes prediction/visualisation code and model installation scripts. There is no useful author-hosted upload demo that I could verify.

This is not a sequence reconstruction model, but it is worth testing as an independent weak signal when two candidate frames have no metadata: does their learned temporal representation agree with the proposed order/period? Never let it override physical sequence evidence.

## Current software signal

Lightroom's June 2026 stacking workflow groups by time or visual similarity and picks a cover; Palomino similarly combines timing and visual similarity for burst grouping. Both show that automatic local grouping is useful. PhotoStar's opportunity is to preserve the *relationship and historical information between frames*, including imperfect frames that conventional culling software encourages users to reject.

## Creative concept — The Moment Between

Once a sequence is established, PhotoStar can create a tiny non-generative story from the frames around a familiar photograph. Instead of animating a single still with invented motion, show the genuine sequence at its natural rhythm: somebody looks away, a dog enters frame, the photographer steps closer, the group relaxes after the posed shot.

This uses only surviving evidence and can make an ordinary roll feel surprisingly alive.

## Experimental concept — Missing Frame

If sequence evidence strongly suggests `12 → 13 → [gap] → 15 → 16`, show the gap.

PhotoStar can search unresolved scans, duplicate folders and disconnected source indexes for a plausible frame 14, but it should never generate it. This materially develops Archive Negative: a missing *position inside a known sequence* is much stronger and more actionable than a generic sparse period.

## Best next experiment

Take 20 known film rolls / digital bursts where the true order is available, temporarily hide that order, and reconstruct using progressively richer evidence:

1. filename/scan/timestamp adjacency;
2. camera/source identity;
3. visual similarity and shared people/place;
4. optional VLM/temporal-embedding evidence.

Measure correct adjacent-pair recovery and, more importantly, the number of false high-confidence links. A useful v1 should prefer leaving a gap over inventing a neighbour.

## Recommendation

**Sequence Memory / Show neighbours is the best next idea to explore.** It is cheap to prototype, strengthens several existing PhotoStar concepts, and extracts historical information already latent in the collection rather than demanding another expensive model. The key rule is: **preserve the order in which photographs were made whenever evidence survives; uncertainty and gaps are part of that order too.**
