## Best next idea — Life Strip

Turn a Person page from “all photos containing this person” into a compact visual model of that person's appearance through life.

A horizontal strip might show representative confirmed faces at ages/periods such as:

`childhood → teens → 20s → 30s → 40s → 50s → later life`

The key is that PhotoStar should not infer all of those ages from appearance. Where a person's birth date and a photograph date/range are known, age is derived deterministically. AI age estimation is only weak evidence for gaps and dating checks.

### User value

- makes a Person page immediately navigable across decades;
- exposes gaps and suspicious identity assignments visually;
- improves recognition by comparing a candidate against age-near exemplars rather than one undifferentiated centroid;
- gives Temporal Triage another independent signal;
- creates a strong, non-generative storytelling surface.

### Effort

Low for a useful UI using existing Person + photo-date data; medium for age-conditioned recognition prototypes; high only if synthetic age transformation is attempted — which is not needed for v1.

## Practical v1 — age bands from evidence we already have

For each confirmed Person appearance:

1. resolve the photograph's accepted date/date range;
2. if the Person has a birth date/range, derive age/range;
3. otherwise leave age unknown rather than guessing;
4. select one stable representative face per useful age/period band;
5. show unresolved candidate faces in a separate “possible appearances” lane.

A representative should favour a clear, reasonably frontal, sufficiently large face, but remain stable once selected so the Person page develops visual landmarks.

The current architecture is unusually suitable: durable Person identity survives machine rebuilds, IdentityClusters remain rebuildable, and weak Face→Person candidates stay separate from explicit decisions.

## Recognition improvement — age-local anchors

The merged people pipeline rebuilds weak Face→Person candidate evidence from accepted anchors. Instead of reducing every confirmed face of Margaret to one conceptual lifetime prototype, keep age-local anchor sets when enough dated evidence exists.

For a candidate photograph probably from 1955, compare most strongly with Margaret's nearby-life confirmed faces while retaining the lifetime match as a second signal. A large disagreement becomes a review reason rather than an automatic rejection.

This should be benchmarked before changing candidate scoring: age, pose, scan quality and model drift are confounded in old family material.

## Product precedent

Ente's current Memory Lane already uses face recognition to build a person's timeline across years and can display age-based captions when a birth date is known. Tonfotos explicitly advertises recognition across age changes and combines People with family relationships. These validate the basic value, but PhotoStar can go further by using the life trajectory as evidence and navigation rather than only as a slideshow/timeline.

- Ente Memory Lane: https://ente.photos/help/photos/features/search-and-discovery/memory-lane
- Tonfotos: https://tonfotos.com/

## Model Lab — MiVOLO

MiVOLO is a practical baseline for age estimation. The authors provide official code, pretrained models and linked Hugging Face live demos; the current repository also advertises MiVOLO-Next live demos. Its published evaluation includes AgeDB, whose identities span substantial age ranges.

Official code/demos: https://github.com/WildChlamydia/MiVOLO

PhotoStar use: not “this person is 37”. Store a noisy age observation/range and compare it against deterministic age implied by Person DOB + photograph date. A large disagreement can flag either the date or identity for review.

## Research Lab — MTLFace

MTLFace explicitly separates identity-related and age-related face representation and jointly studies age-invariant recognition and face-age synthesis. The authors provide official PyTorch code and dataset/annotation resources:

- official code: https://github.com/Hzzone/MTLFace
- author project page: https://hzzone.github.io/MTLFace/
- paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01520

This is older research rather than a production recommendation, but its decomposition is conceptually useful: PhotoStar wants identity to remain stable while age becomes useful evidence rather than noise.

I would test its recognition ideas only in Model Lab. Synthetic aged faces should never become archive evidence.

## Current research watch — HMLF

The April 2026 paper “Age-Invariant Face Retrieval Based on Hybrid Metric Learning Framework” targets the 1:N retrieval problem rather than only pairwise verification and combines ArcFace with supervised contrastive learning without requiring age labels.

Paper: https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/15/9/1851

## UI — Life Strip + evidence lanes

A Person page could have three layers:

**Life Strip** — one confirmed representative per period.

**Photographs** — normal chronological gallery, jumping to the selected period.

**Possible appearances** — unresolved Face→Person candidates, preferably positioned near the period they would imply.

That makes an anomaly obvious: a supposed Margaret face from 1938 appears visually between her 1970s and 1980s representatives. PhotoStar can ask for review without pretending appearance alone proves an error.

## Creative precedent — The Brown Sisters

Nicholas Nixon's *The Brown Sisters* has photographed the same four sisters annually since 1975, retaining their left-to-right order. MoMA describes the passage of time as the defining force across the series. It demonstrates how powerful a simple repeated human presence can be without animation, generated aging or elaborate narration.

MoMA: https://www.moma.org/explore/inside_out/2014/12/05/nicholas-nixon-40-years-of-the-brown-sisters/

PhotoStar obviously lacks controlled annual portraits. That imperfection is interesting: it can assemble the accidental family equivalent from birthdays, holidays, snapshots and formal portraits.

## Creative concept — One Face, One Life

Generate a deliberately restrained sequence containing one real confirmed face crop per year/period, aligned only enough that the eyes occupy roughly the same position. Fade or step between genuine source photographs.

No generated intermediate faces. No fake aging. Clicking any face opens the original photograph and evidence.

This could be an unusually strong Memory Bundle/export because the archive itself supplies the aging process.

## Experimental concept — Identity discontinuity detector

For a confirmed Person, compare neighbouring age bands. If a set of faces forms a strong internal cluster but has weak continuity with both earlier and later confirmed appearances, flag:

> This part of Margaret's Life Strip may contain a mistaken identity.

Conversely, an unresolved face that bridges two known age periods could become a high-value One Good Question candidate.

This is not a biometric truth engine. Its job is to find where a human review has unusually high value.

## Best next experiment

Use 10–20 confirmed people with long photographic histories.

1. Build Life Strips using only accepted photo dates + known birth dates.
2. Select stable representative faces per 5/10-year band.
3. Measure current candidate similarity against (a) all lifetime anchors and (b) age-near anchors.
4. Deliberately inject several wrong identities and see whether trajectory discontinuity ranks them highly.
5. Separately run MiVOLO on the same faces and measure whether its noisy age estimates add useful review evidence.

The important metric is not age-estimation MAE. It is: **does life-aware evidence reduce false Person matches and make identity/date mistakes easier for a human to spot?**

## Recommendation

Life Strip is the best next idea today because the newly merged People architecture finally makes it cheap and safe to build. Start with deterministic ages and navigation, not another AI pipeline. If that UI proves useful, age-local recognition and age-estimation evidence can be added experimentally behind it.

Design rule: **use time to strengthen identity; never manufacture a face to fill a missing year.**
