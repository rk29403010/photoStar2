### Feature: Room Atlas

A user opens a photograph of Christmas 1974, identifies the living room and clicks **Other photographs in this room**. PhotoStar shows the same room in 1968, 1974 and 1986, including viewpoints with different furniture and people.

The user can choose **Same room**, **Different room** or **Uncertain**. A human-confirmed room is an entity separate from the broader location or building; a room might have been remodelled, moved to a different function, or photographed from opposite corners.

A useful first UI is a split panel: selected photograph on the left, room-specific contact sheet on the right, with a decade slider and an **Unmatched interiors** queue. Show candidate evidence (fireplace, door/window layout, wallpaper, furniture, family annotations). Preserve originals and source attribution.

**Effort estimates:** manual room identity + photo links, 3–5 developer days; contact sheet and timeline filters, 3–5 days; candidate matching and evidence display, 5–10 days, subject to available embeddings. These are provisional estimates, not measured.

**Important:** the same wallpaper or furniture is not proof of the same room. A photograph of a framed picture or television screen is also not evidence that the depicted space is the room where the camera stood. A room's layout may change over decades.

### Research and demos

**[VGGT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11651), CVPR 2025.** Estimates camera geometry, depth and 3D point maps from one or multiple images. [Official authors' code](https://github.com/facebookresearch/vggt); [official Meta/author Hugging Face demo](https://huggingface.co/spaces/facebook/vggt) (listed Running on Zero, interactive inference not tested). It could explore multi-view interiors, but plausible single-image geometry must not be mistaken for a measured historical floorplan.

**[MASt3R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09756), ECCV 2024.** Learns image correspondences robust to large viewpoint changes. [Official NAVER code](https://github.com/naver/mast3r); [official NAVER Hugging Face demo](https://huggingface.co/spaces/naver/MASt3R) (listed Running on Zero, inference not tested). Its code is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; check licensing before production integration.

**[COLMAP](https://colmap.github.io/).** Conventional multi-view Structure-from-Motion and stereo, useful as a baseline when multiple photographs have genuine overlap. [Official code](https://github.com/colmap/colmap), with runnable GUI/CLI and examples; no official hosted upload-and-reconstruct demo verified. Older family photographs are frequently too sparse, blurred or different in content for reliable geometry.

No model should be introduced until a small test demonstrates an improvement over existing scene features and manual confirmation.

### Art, context and experimental concept

The [Museum of the Home’s Rooms Through Time](https://museumofthehome.org.uk/whats-on/rooms-through-time/a-terraced-house-in-1978/) uses reconstructed domestic spaces to connect objects, design and social history. Its [Kenneth Lam *Still Living* series](https://museumofthehome.org.uk/museum-of-the-home-commissions-artist-kenneth-lam-in-new-photography-series-still-living/) (2025) interprets period rooms through carefully composed still lifes. [Milton Rogovin's family interior photographs](https://www.nationalgalleries.org/art-and-artists/165755/family-miners-1981-1990-scotland) similarly show how a room reveals social context beyond the people.

**Experimental: The House That Changed.** A story mode presents actual photographs of one room in chronological order, with optional sourced historical reference cards for furniture, wallpaper, appliances and domestic habits. A ghosted overlay shows *verified* recurring features, while unknown parts remain blank. If multi-view reconstruction succeeds, an optional 3D exploratory model could link camera viewpoints; it must be labelled a visual interpretation, not an authenticated reconstruction. Modern photographs could show the surviving space without claiming unchanged geometry.

### Best next experiment

Pick 80–120 interior photographs from 5–10 known rooms, with deliberate distractors (similar wallpaper, identical furniture, remodelled rooms). Manually label ground truth. Compare existing scene-feature retrieval against human-guided room matching, then try MASt3R/VGGT only on unresolved cases. Measure precision of same-room matches, top-five retrieval, false merges, human review time and benefit from a confirmed room across the library.

**Recommendation:** build **Same room / Other photographs here** and a compact room contact sheet first. It offers immediate browsing value, is reversible, and does not require a GPU, model download or another complicated background pipeline.
