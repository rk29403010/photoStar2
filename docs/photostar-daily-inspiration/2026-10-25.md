## The Moment Picker — keep the story, not just the sharpest frame

**Best next idea:** a sequence-aware review panel that compares near-identical frames, recommends a gallery representative, and keeps the surrounding frames available as historical evidence. Unlike ordinary AI culling, its goal is not to delete rejected photographs.

### Product concept

Opening a capture-sequence stack shows a **contact sheet** of its frames with synchronised zoom, arrows for quick comparisons, and face crops for each detected person. A user can set a preferred **gallery representative**, but the original sequence and all files remain intact.

A proposed recommendation should give *two independent reasons*:
1. **Technically stronger:** focus, exposure, closed eyes, motion blur, framing.
2. **Historically or emotionally distinctive:** a person appears only in this frame, a gesture changes, a sign is legible, or a spontaneous interaction tells more of the story.

The cleanest frame is not always the most valuable. A blurry photo may be the only photograph of someone at that event.

### Practical roadmap and provisional effort

| Priority | Improvement | User value | Estimated focused effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Sequence Contact Sheet**: open existing group with 4–12 thumbnails, keyboard navigation and linked zoom | Review groups without repetitive open/close actions | 2–4 days |
| 2 | **Choose representative**: reversible, human-owned selection distinct from auto quality rank | Cleaner large-library gallery, no loss of originals | 2–4 days |
| 3 | **Face comparison strip**: corresponding face crops across frames | Quickly spot blinking, expression changes and sharpness | 3–6 days |
| 4 | **Why this frame?** separate quality and uniqueness signals, with uncertain labels | Explain recommendations and preserve rare content | 3–6 days |
| Later | **Sequence playback** and narrative captions with source frame links | Make the seconds around a moment explorable | Medium |

**Data rule:** distinguish the original capture sequence from a scan-order series; do not turn filename adjacency into a claimed capture chronology. Preserve a user-selected representative separately from rebuildable AI suggestions, and never silently delete other frames. A representative should be stable when model scores change.

### Market scan — complementary lessons, not a product clone

- [Adobe Lightroom Classic](https://helpx.adobe.com/lightroom-classic/desktop/viewing-photos/browse-compare-photos.html) has Compare/Survey views and linked zoom. Good low-friction review precedent.
- [Aftershoot](https://support.aftershoot.com/en/articles/6508163-setting-your-ai-automated-culling-preferences-in-aftershoot) (guide updated 3 September 2026) sorts selected, duplicate, blurry and closed-eye frames. Useful quality cues; PhotoStar should retain historically important outtakes rather than prioritising delivery-volume reduction.
- [Narrative Select](https://narrative.so/select/the-close-ups-panel) provides per-face close-ups and assessments, especially useful for group photographs. Its [documentation](https://help.narrative.so/en/articles/7337371-zoom-and-close-ups-in-narrative) warns that not every background face is detected or assessed.
- [digiKam](https://docs.digikam.org/en/) offers a Light Table, image versions, ratings and similarity tools; useful inspiration for transparent source/variant separation.
- [MyHeritage photo tools](https://education.myheritage.com/article/myheritage-photo-features/) distinguish restored from generated or colourised results with visible indicators. A PhotoStar montage or composite should likewise be explicitly labelled.
- [FamilySearch Memories](https://www.familysearch.org/en/memories/) connects family images and recollections; a chosen representative should be a navigation preference, not a replacement for the event's complete photographic record.

### Research / model lab

**TOPIQ (2023), no-reference image quality assessment.** Semantic-aware quality assessment might identify important local defects missed by an overall sharpness measure. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03060); [official authors' code and local inference examples](https://github.com/chaofengc/IQA-PyTorch). No verified first-party hosted upload demo was found. **Caution:** toolbox/model licensing needs checking before production packaging; quality predictions are not measures of family value.

**MUSIQ (ICCV 2021), multi-scale image quality.** Its handling of multiple scales may help rank full-frame technical quality without reducing everything to a small thumbnail. [Google Research explanation](https://research.google/blog/musiq-assessing-image-aesthetic-and-technical-quality-with-multi-scale-transformers/); [official Google Research code, checkpoints and usage](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/master/musiq/README.md); [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05997). No verified official hosted upload demo found. The research code is a baseline, not a Windows-ready app plugin.

**SER-FIQ (CVPR 2020), face image quality for recognition.** Potentially useful as a *separate* face-quality signal, but it measures usefulness to a face recogniser rather than whether someone looks happy or whether a photo is historically valuable. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.09373); [official authors' repository](https://github.com/pterhoer/FaceImageQuality); [official runnable example](https://github.com/pterhoer/FaceImageQuality/blob/master/serfiq_example.py). No verified hosted first-party interactive demo. The authors warn about model dependence, and the code is noncommercially licensed with older MXNet dependencies.

**Start without a new model:** use existing photo quality categories and face regions, plus simple crop-level focus/exposure heuristics; only test these models if they outperform a human-reviewed baseline on real family sequences.

### Art / storytelling

[Magnum Photos' contact sheets](https://store.magnumphotos.com/collections/contact-sheets) and the publisher's [Magnum Contact Sheets](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/products/magnum-contact-sheets) show how adjacent frames and photographers' selection marks reveal the making of a famous image. This is a useful storytelling precedent: a family's accidental, imperfect frames may show the movement and emotion that the formal portrait missed.

**Experimental concept — The Seconds Around It:** a gentle filmstrip playback that animates *navigation* across genuine adjacent exposures, with source frame and uncertain ordering always visible. It is not AI video generation and must not invent intermediate actions. If a user chooses to create an interpolated video later, label it a derived artistic reconstruction, not a historical recording.

### Best next experiment

Select 30 known groups of 3–15 frames, mixing phone bursts, scanned negatives and filename-only candidate series. Ask a person to choose a preferred display frame and mark any uniquely valuable outtakes. Compare against the current technical-quality ordering and a simple face-crop heuristic.

Measure: representative agreement; review seconds per group; missed unique people/details; false sequence merges; gallery stability; CPU and memory. **Never optimise solely for the fraction of photos hidden.**

**Recommendation:** build the **read-only Sequence Contact Sheet** first, then the persistent human-selected representative. This is a small, immediately useful development of PhotoStar's existing sequence architecture and avoids new model/deployment risk.
