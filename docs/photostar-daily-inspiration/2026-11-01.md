# PhotoStar2 Daily Inspiration — 1 November 2026 — Companion Threads

> Research/product inspiration, not an approved roadmap. No application code changed.

## Best next idea: Companion Threads — pets and working animals as first-class archive identities

PhotoStar's existing People, related-photo and evidence systems focus on humans. Family dogs, cats, horses and other animals often recur across decades of photographs, but generic object tags cannot express **the same individual animal**. A dog photographed with several generations of people can link photographs that are otherwise difficult to connect. This is a new entity and navigation dimension, not a request to train a dog-face model immediately.

**First version:** create a human-confirmed animal profile (name, species, optional life-date ranges, distinguishing markings, original source photos). In the single-photo viewer, select an animal region and choose **Same animal as… / Different animal / Unsure**. Keep suggestions, rejected matches and accepted links distinct; don't silently equate two similar-looking dogs. From a profile, open a **Companion Timeline** of genuine photographs, with people, places and events shown as optional contextual filters.

**Illustrative use:** An old family snapshot includes a dog. Confirming it is the same dog as one in a different album could link two gatherings, but must not by itself prove a common date or location. Animals age, change coat, may wear identical collars, and multiple similar dogs may coexist.

## Practical design and effort (provisional focused development estimates)

| Change | User value | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add `animal` semantic entity, stable region-to-animal relationship and human decisions | Preserve individual identity separately from species/breed tags | 3–5 days |
| Viewer **This animal** / **Same animal?** actions, plus a Companion contact sheet | Browse all photos of one pet without manual albums | 3–5 days |
| Gallery **Animals** lens: named, unidentified, disputed | Navigate a large archive through recurring companions | 2–4 days |
| Suggestions from existing object/scene observations, bounded by date and album context | Prioritise likely matches for review; no extra model | 3–6 days |
| Optional animal-specific embedding benchmark | Improve difficult cases, only if measurable | Research + 5–12 days integration |

Start with manual region boxes and human-confirmed links. Animal identity must not inherit the human face-recognition score or treat a breed classification as an individual match. Preserve the original image and region geometry; source evidence should survive re-analysis. Distinguish **same individual**, **same species/breed**, **similar-looking**, and **unknown**.

## Software sampled

- [Immich facial recognition](https://docs.immich.app/features/facial-recognition/): names and merges *human* face clusters, and lets users browse photos by person. Useful interaction precedent, but not evidence of animal support.
- [digiKam People View](https://docs.digikam.org/en/left_sidebar/people_view.html): Unknown/Unconfirmed/Confirmed human-face workflow and reversible manual labels. Its docs caution that an incorrect confirmation can affect subsequent suggestions; animal matches need the same caution.
- [Tropy selections](https://docs.tropy.org/in-the-item-view/selections): annotate a specific image region and attach independent notes. Good region-level UX precedent.
- [Huply](https://www.huply.com/): pet-specific photo journal with chronological records; suggests the value of a pet timeline but is not an archival evidence system.
- [Gramps Web](https://www.grampsweb.org/user-guide/tasks/): family-history task and evidence-oriented workflows. Animal identity should be linked to human households/events without pretending animals are GEDCOM people.
- [Topaz Photo](https://docs.topazlabs.com/topaz-photo/release-summary): current restoration/editor reference (v1.7.1 released 22 October 2026). PhotoStar should match animals on source pixels, not AI-reconstructed fur or faces.

## Research and official code/demo checks

**PetFace — Shinoda & Shiohara, ECCV 2024**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13555 . The [authors' project page](https://dahlian00.github.io/PetFacePage/) includes an **official demonstration video** and links to the [official implementation](https://github.com/mapooon/PetFace). It benchmarks animal face re-identification across 13 animal families, including dogs and cats. **Limitations:** non-commercial research-only code/models; dataset access is gated; alignment and preprocessing add deployment complexity. No verified official hosted interactive upload demo was found.

**WildlifeDatasets / MegaDescriptor — Čermák et al., WACV 2024**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09118 . Official [dataset/evaluation code](https://github.com/WildlifeDatasets/wildlife-datasets), [WildlifeTools inference/examples](https://github.com/WildlifeDatasets/wildlife-tools), and [authors' small MegaDescriptor-T weights with runnable Python example](https://huggingface.co/BVRA/MegaDescriptor-T-224) exist. The tool library is MIT, but the selected model weights are **CC BY-NC 4.0** and the datasets have independent restrictions. No verified first-party hosted interactive upload demo; Hugging Face lists third-party Spaces, which must not be represented as author demos. A generic wildlife model may perform poorly on historical domestic dogs.

**Implementation judgement:** do not integrate either now. First benchmark existing observations and manual regions on 100–200 known animal photographs; compare top-five recall, false merges, age/coat-change robustness, processing time and licensing/Windows packaging. Test different individuals of the same breed deliberately.

## Art and storytelling — The Animals Who Were There

British artist [Keith Arnatt's *Walking the Dog* (1976–79)](https://thephotographersgallery.org.uk/whats-on/rubbish-and-recollections-keith-arnatt) treats the person–dog pair as a portrait of everyday life, not just an animal photograph. The [Morgan Library's William Wegman interview](https://www.themorgan.org/videos/company-animals-william-wegman-dogs-and-photography) similarly explores what animal companions reveal about human relationships.

**Experimental concept — The Household Seen Through Its Animals.** Select a confirmed pet and see a timeline of its appearances, with the people and homes around it changing over the years. A pet may link a family house before and after renovation, a child's early and later life, or the arrival of a new generation. Overlay genuine family recollections and optional sourced historical context about domestic animals, never invented behaviour or thoughts. Where dates are uncertain, use ranges rather than a falsely precise lifespan. The output could be a small photographic essay, *Who Was There With Us?*

## Best next experiment

Use ~120 photographs featuring at least 4 known animals, including 2 visually similar animals and changes across age, grooming and image quality. Start with manual regions and **Same animal / Different animal / Unsure**. Measure (a) additional photographs found per confirmed animal, (b) false merges, (c) time to find a known pet photograph, and (d) whether animal browsing uncovers otherwise disconnected family events. Only add a model if it beats the existing object/related-photo baseline.

**Recommendation:** Build the **human-confirmed animal identity and Companion contact sheet**, not animal face recognition. This is a genuinely new navigation axis with modest initial complexity, and it fits PhotoStar's source-aware architecture.
