## Best next idea: Rescue Radar — a preservation triage view

PhotoStar2 can become more than a photo finder and restorer: it can show which *originals* are actually at risk, and suggest the lowest-effort way to protect them. A missing or corrupt original is more consequential than a poor caption. Distinguish **digital integrity** (can bytes be read and verified), **representation quality** (is this only a thumbnail/low-resolution copy?), **historical value** (is it a unique family image?) and **backup coverage** (is an independently recoverable copy known to exist?).

The best first implementation is a **read-only Archive Health scan**, not a new model or a backup service.

## Proposed UX — Archive Health / Rescue Radar

A compact status strip above the existing gallery:
- **Verified original** — original is readable and a previous checksum baseline matches.
- **Unverified** — not yet checked; *not* equivalent to damaged.
- **Attention needed** — decode failure, missing file, checksum change, or sole low-resolution copy.
- **Recovery candidate** — another likely copy exists, but equivalence and quality must be reviewed.
- **Backup unknown** — do not infer that a second path or duplicate is an independent backup.

The gallery should filter on these states without duplicating or reordering photographs. An issue drawer should say *why* the image is flagged and suggest a reversible action: check another drive, compare two versions, rescan a print, or verify a backup. Use aggregate progress counts but no invented percentages of "safe" photographs.

### Priority model

1. Immediate: missing file, unreadable master, verified checksum mismatch.
2. High: unique image with only a low-resolution copy or a single unverified storage location.
3. Medium: uncertain provenance or unusual format; needs review rather than automatic conversion.
4. Low: ordinary scan quality improvements and optional aesthetic restoration.

The list is *action priority*, not an invented probability of future loss. Avoid ranking emotional importance by automated visual scores.

### Implementation outline and effort

| Improvement | User value | Rough focused effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read-only scan: existence, file size, decode status, dimensions, format, scan time | Finds actionable faults without touching originals | 2–4 days |
| Streaming SHA-256 baseline and periodic recheck with an immutable observation ledger | Detects unexpected changes after baseline; does not identify which copy is correct | 3–6 days |
| Issue-focused Archive Health filter with reason, evidence and acknowledgement | Makes very large collections manageable | 3–5 days |
| Review candidate copies using existing variant/group evidence | Helps recover originals without deleting variants | 4–8 days |
| Opt-in export of checksum manifest / BagIt package | Portable preservation evidence independent of PhotoStar | 3–6 days |
| Restoration comparison assistant (later) | Helps select useful output while preserving the source | Research-dependent |

Use node:crypto streaming hashes and existing sharp decoding; throttle I/O, support pause/resume, avoid full-file buffering, and log errors without changing source files. Checksum verification must compare to a *previous trusted baseline*; hashing a corrupt file for the first time cannot detect historic corruption. Backups must be independently restorable; identical hashes on the same failing disk are not redundancy. Never auto-delete "duplicates" or replace an original with an AI restoration.

## Current software and preservation practice

- [PhotoStructure](https://photostructure.com/features/) groups duplicates and variants, watches multiple drives and discusses damaged files. Its [October 15, 2026 deduplication guidance](https://photostructure.com/guide/what-do-you-mean-by-deduplicate/) is a useful current reference. PhotoStar can differentiate by explaining *risk and recovery evidence* rather than just collapsing variants.
- [digiKam 9.2 maintenance tools](https://docs.digikam.org/en/maintenance_tools/maintenance_overview.html) provide duplicate finding, database cleaning, thumbnail rebuilding and quality scanning. PhotoStar can consolidate these maintenance concerns into a small, prioritised, non-destructive review queue.
- [Tropy](https://www.tropy.org/) preserves archival notes and region-specific research context. PhotoStar should retain annotations and testimony even when the linked image file goes missing.
- [FamilySearch Memories, updated May 13, 2026](https://www.familysearch.org/en/help/helpcenter/article/how-do-i-upload-photos-or-documents-to-memories) accepts family photographs and stories, but an upload service is not a verified independent preservation strategy.
- [Library of Congress recommended still-image formats](https://www.loc.gov/preservation/resources/rfs/stillimg.html) prioritise the best available representation, original resolution and colour information. Do not convert everything to TIFF indiscriminately or discard original JPEG/RAW.
- [NDSA Levels of Digital Preservation v2.1, March 23, 2026](https://www.ndsa.org/2026/03/23/announcing-version-2_1-of-the-ndsa-levels-of-digital-preservation.html) adds sustainability considerations; [April 30, 2026 fixity survey](https://www.ndsa.org/2026/04/30/2025-ndsa-fixity-survey-report-published.html) found checksum use common and reported corrupt streams/interrupted transfers among observed errors. Favour incremental checks over gratuitous repeated full-disk scans.

## Libraries, models and research — official links and demos

**1. BagIt — portable archive manifests.** [Official Library of Congress code](https://github.com/LibraryOfCongress/bagit-python), with command-line examples for SHA-256 manifest creation and validation. The [BagIt format](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8493) is documented in RFC 8493. *Demo:* runnable official CLI examples; no official hosted interactive demo found. Potential export standard, not a required runtime dependency.

**2. JHOVE — format validation.** [Official Open Preservation Foundation code](https://github.com/openpreserve/jhove) includes JPEG, TIFF and other format modules and a local CLI/GUI. *Demo:* official runnable local CLI/GUI, no verified hosted interactive demo. Useful as an optional deep validation tool, not necessary for first-pass sharp checks. Packaging a Java runtime on Windows would increase maintenance.

**3. FGResQ (AAAI 2026) — restoration comparison.** [Paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/37846); [official authors' code](https://github.com/sxfly99/FGResQ); [official project page](https://sxfly99.github.io/FGResQ-Home/); [author-linked Hugging Face demo](https://huggingface.co/spaces/orpheus0429/FGResQ). Authors released updated v2 weights on 3 June 2026 and support single-image scoring and pairwise restored-image ranking. *Demo status:* official Space exists but was **Sleeping** when checked 2 November; no inference tested. This judges perceptual restoration quality, **not historical accuracy** or file integrity. Potential later comparison benchmark, not a basis for deleting originals.

**4. Revisiting Vision–Language Foundations for No-Reference Image Quality Assessment (WACV 2026).** [Official authors' code and paper links](https://github.com/drkkgy/NR_IQA_AGM). Tests CLIP, SigLIP2 and other backbones, with reproducible local evaluation and pretrained checkpoints. *Demo:* official local evaluation scripts; no verified author-hosted interactive upload demo. Use only to benchmark image-quality triage, not determine archival worth.

**5. DiAGRAM — UK National Archives preservation-risk research.** [Official interactive tool](https://diagram.nationalarchives.gov.uk/); [official project source and runbook](https://nationalarchives.github.io/DiAGRAM/). It uses a Bayesian network to explore archive-wide risks, but asks institutional questions and does not diagnose individual damaged photos. Its what-if methodology could inspire a future user-facing "what would one verified backup change?" simulation, with no fabricated risk percentages.

## Creative concept — The Material Biography

Artist [Christian Boltanski's Réserve des Suisses morts](https://www.mcba.ch/en/collection/reserve-des-suisses-morts-reserve-of-dead-swiss/) shows how photographic material and presentation carry memory beyond the image content.

A **Material Biography** could show one photograph in three linked views: the original scan (including creases, edges and writing), a restrained restored reading copy, and the evidence trail of how the photograph survived (album, rescanning, migration, family recollection). Show provenance and *real* preservation events only. This is not a synthetic reconstruction of missing history. It extends prior album/reverse-side concepts by treating preservation and repair decisions as part of the photograph's biography.
