# PhotoStar2 Daily Inspiration — 3 November 2026 — Family Time Constraints

> Product research and an implementation proposal, not an approved roadmap. No application code changed.

## Best next idea — Family Time Constraints

Use an imported family tree as **evidence for who could plausibly appear in a photograph**, not as an authority that automatically names faces. PhotoStar already has confirmed identities, date estimates, provenance-aware claims and reversible semantic decisions. Its [People backlog](https://github.com/rk29403010/photoStar2/blob/main/docs/todo.md) explicitly requests GEDCOM import and family-history-informed identification. The new element is a practical **chronology/relationship constraint engine**, beginning with a read-only import preview and candidate review.

### Concrete example

An undated photograph is estimated to have been taken between 1958 and 1968. A confirmed subject is someone's grandfather; another unknown subject appears to be a teenager. A GEDCOM tree might narrow plausible candidates to relatives alive during that period, and show who would have been approximately 12–20 years old. Display these as hypotheses, not identity assignments.

The date estimate is not proof; neither are estimated ages, genealogy imports or inferred family relationships. Strong constraints (a reliably documented birth after the photograph, or death before it) can reject a candidate only when the photo date and vital record are sufficiently reliable. Uncertain date ranges should widen possibilities rather than produce false exclusions. Relationships such as adoption, stepfamilies and informal kinship should be represented explicitly.

## UX — Identity Possibilities drawer

In the existing single-photo viewer, selecting an unknown face opens:

| Candidate | Evidence in favour | Evidence against | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mary (born c. 1945) | Age range fits; related to confirmed person | No direct visual match | Possible / reject |
| Joan (born 1957) | Family relation plausible | Looks older than expected (weak signal) | Possible / reject |
| Albert (born 1974) | Similar family name | Birth postdates reliable 1962 photo | Exclude if dates verified |

Illustrative records only. Show *why* candidates appear and what assumptions drive them. Provide **Same person**, **Not this person**, **Uncertain**. Persist human decisions independently of import generations and AI recomputation.

For large collections, add a **Time & Family** filter to the People view: unknown faces grouped by estimated decade and connected family branch; use an uncertainty ribbon for photographs with broad date intervals. Do not automatically merge faces or silently update confirmed names.

## Practical increments and estimated effort

| Increment | Benefit | Effort (working days, provisional) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GEDCOM import dry-run and validation report | Inspect names, dates, families, source/encoding issues before any database write | 3–6 |
| Imported-person provenance layer | Preserve source file, import generation, source identifiers, date precision, relationships | 4–8 |
| Time-constraint candidate generator | Prune impossible candidates, rank plausible ones with transparent reasons | 4–8 |
| Identity Possibilities drawer | Review suggestions against existing face regions and testimony | 3–6 |
| Family/decade browsing lens | Navigate large collections through plausible life periods | 4–7 |
| Optional age-model benchmark | Determine whether visual age adds useful recall beyond dates/relations | Research-dependent |

**Architecture:** start with GEDCOM 5.5.1 and 7.0 `.ged` detection; stage `.gdz` support separately. Preserve original source and extensions, don't overwrite existing PhotoStar person records, and make repeated imports idempotent by source + external ID + version. Map imprecise dates to intervals, not a fake exact day. Candidate generation should be batch/rebuildable, with provenance links, rather than synchronous vector searches on every click.

## Research and products sampled

- [FamilySearch GEDCOM 7.0.18 specification (17 February 2026)](https://gedcom.io/specifications/FamilySearchGEDCOMv7.html) and [official specification repository](https://github.com/FamilySearch/GEDCOM): authoritative format, including date phrases, sources and GEDZIP; the `v7.1` branch is still a draft.
- [Gramps Web](https://www.grampsweb.org/features/) offers genealogy charts, timelines and a **dry-run import preview**; its [import guidance](https://www.grampsweb.org/administration/import/) notes encoding, custom tags and broken media paths. Borrow the dry-run pattern, not a whole genealogy interface.
- [RootsMagic](https://www.rootsmagic.com/) shows why users expect to retain their family tree locally; PhotoStar can be a photo-first complement rather than another full genealogy editor.
- [Immich People](https://docs.immich.app/features/facial-recognition/) supports dates of birth, person merging and searching; [digiKam People](https://docs.digikam.org/en/left_sidebar/people_view.html) offers confirmed/unconfirmed face-review workflows. PhotoStar can add historical constraint explanations.
- [MyHeritage PhotoDater](https://www.myheritage.com/help/en/articles/12852507-what-is-photodater-and-how-does-it-work), documented 21 July 2026, reports about 60% of its supported historical photo estimates within five years; it **does not** silently save estimated dates. Proprietary service; no public official model code found. This is not a benchmark for PhotoStar's own dataset.
- [Topaz Photo releases](https://docs.topazlabs.com/topaz-photo/release-summary) include v1.7.1 on 22 October 2026. Keep original photographic evidence separate from AI-restored faces when testing identity candidates.

### Tools/models worth testing (not immediate dependencies)

**GEDCOM parsing:** [`js-gedcom` by Luther Tychonievich](https://github.com/gedcom7code/js-gedcom) is an independently authored, permissively licensed JavaScript parser/validator; its [author-provided browser validator](https://gedcom7code.github.io/js-gedcom/) processes files locally. This is **not** the FamilySearch official implementation. It assumes bytes have already been decoded into text, so 5.5.1 ANSEL/legacy encodings need separate handling.

**Age estimation:** [MiVOLO paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04616), [official authors' code](https://github.com/WildChlamydia/MiVOLO), [official authors' running Hugging Face demo](https://huggingface.co/spaces/iitolstykh/MiVOLO-Demo), and [authors' model card](https://huggingface.co/iitolstykh/mivolo_v2). Useful only as a *weak*, wide-interval ranking feature. Reported mean absolute errors of several years are on particular benchmark datasets, not faded archival scans; do not infer gender or kinship from appearance.

**Probabilistic record linkage:** [Splink paper (2022)](https://ijpds.org/article/view/1794), [official UK Ministry of Justice code](https://github.com/moj-analytical-services/splink), [official interactive tutorial/visualisation documentation](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/getting_started.html). [Splink 5 released 28 September 2026](https://moj-analytical-services.github.io/splink/blog/2026/09/28/splink-500-released.html), improving incremental linkage and reducing dependencies. Good research baseline for matching imported people to existing biographical records, but likely excessive for a first import; official local runnable examples and browser-based documentation exist, no verified first-party hosted upload-and-match demo.

## Experimental storytelling — Parallel Lives

Inspired by [Nicholas Nixon's *The Brown Sisters*](https://www.fundacionmapfre.org/en/art-and-culture/exhibitions/historical/year-2025/the-brown-sisters/), a long-running annual family portrait sequence, offer a **Same Age, Different Generation** view. Select age 25, then see actual photographs of relatives at roughly that age, aligned against the year they lived it. Overlay *sourced* historical context (e.g. housing, technology, public events), not generated appearances. For uncertain dates, show a band or exclude the photograph from exact-age comparisons. Unlike earlier person timelines, this compares **generations at equivalent life stages**.

## Test and recommendation

Test a synthetic GEDCOM file with 100–300 people and 30–50 photographs whose identities/dates are independently known. Include approximate dates, duplicate names, adoption/stepfamilies, missing death dates, changed surnames, inconsistent records and erroneous face-age estimates. Measure candidate recall@5, false hard exclusions, review time and re-import idempotence. **Zero false exclusions based solely on a visual age estimate** is a key acceptance condition.

**Build the GEDCOM dry-run + source-preserving import first**, then a non-destructive Identity Possibilities drawer. That delivers a planned PhotoStar capability without adding another AI model or full genealogy editor. Revisit age estimation only if it measurably improves candidate retrieval on historical images.
